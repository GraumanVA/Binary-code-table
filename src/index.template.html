Methods

The table shows the strongest bounds among the enabled sources and their documented parity and shortening consequences.

How bounds are calculated
  • Archive rows: The format n d k N Type Ref gives A(n,d) ≥ N·2^k.
  • Missing lengths: Shorten from the next eligible archived length, rounding the resulting lower bound upward to an integer.
  • Parity: A(n,2t−1) = A(n+1,2t) relates odd and even distances. The table shows only odd distances by default to avoid duplication. Even-distance source bounds still contribute to their equivalent odd-distance entries.
  • Merging: Take the largest lower bound and smallest upper bound. General bounds use Singleton, Hamming, Plotkin, and classical Johnson. Johnson uses a nested-floor constant-weight bound; even distances use the parity identity. Equivalent odd/even pairs share their strongest elementary upper bound. The smallest upper bound wins.

Archive corrections and attribution

The erroneous Litsyn lower bounds at (32,19) and (35,21) are excluded from calculations, including parity and shortening. Each claims 8 codewords, exceeding the Plotkin upper bound of 6. The original archive rows are retained in the source data.
